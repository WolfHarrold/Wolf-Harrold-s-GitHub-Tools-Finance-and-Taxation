Questions

Common questions

What is the "basic rate"?

The basic rate is the core part of a payment before supplements. Centrelink adds supplements, such as the Energy Supplement and Pension Supplement, to make the maximum rate. The income test and assets test then reduce the maximum rate. This tool shows each part.

How does the income test work?

Each payment has an income free area. Income below it does not change the payment. Above it, each extra dollar reduces the payment by a set amount: 50c or 60c for JobSeeker, 40c for Parenting Payment Single and principal carers, and 50c for pensions.

Income is gross, before tax. Report it for the fortnight you earned it.

Which test applies, income or assets?

Centrelink works out the rate under both tests and pays the lower result. For allowances such as JobSeeker, the assets test is a limit: above it, no payment. For pensions, the payment reduces by $3 a fortnight for each $1,000 over the limit.

What is deeming?

Centrelink assumes bank accounts, shares and other financial investments earn a set rate. From 20 September 2026 the rates are 1.75% on the first $66,800 (single) and 3.75% above. Enter the value of the investments, not the interest you get.

What is the Work Bonus?

For people over Age Pension age, the first $300 a fortnight of pay from work is not counted. Unused amounts build up to $11,800. This tool applies the $300 a fortnight only. It does not use a saved-up balance.

Does a partner's income matter?

Yes. For pensions, the couple's income and assets are added together. For JobSeeker and Parenting Payment Partnered, the partner's income above $1,440 a fortnight reduces the payment by 60c for each dollar.

Why is my real payment different?

This is an estimate. Waiting periods, the Income Maintenance Period, Working Credit, the income bank, compensation, overseas pensions and the Work Bonus balance can change the real amount. Use the Payment and Service Finder or your Centrelink online account for the exact figure.

Method

How this calculator works

Steps

  1. Build the maximum rate. Basic rate, plus supplements, plus Rent Assistance for people without children.
  2. Count income. Pay from work (less the Work Bonus for pensioners), other income and deemed income from investments.
  3. Apply the income test. Take away the reduction for income above the free area, and for partner income where it applies.
  4. Apply the assets test. Compare assets with the limit for a homeowner or non-homeowner.
  5. Pay the lower result. The rate is the lower of the two tests.

Not included

  • Youth Allowance, Austudy, ABSTUDY and Special Benefit
  • Waiting periods and the Income Maintenance Period
  • Working Credit, the student income bank and the Work Bonus balance
  • Partners who get a pension when the claimant gets an allowance
  • Over Age Pension age rules for allowances
  • Remote Area Allowance and other add-ons
